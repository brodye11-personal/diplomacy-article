# Tone of Voice — Diplomacy article

**Built from:** `essay.md` · **Medium:** long-form LessWrong / AI Alignment Forum article · **Last updated:** 2026-07-20

## One-line summary
Conversational, morally serious, and technically precise: open with a concrete strategic vignette, turn it into a sharp claim, then explain the mechanism in plain language.

## Tone & register
Write for an intelligent sceptic as a peer. Be direct and decisive about the central argument, but name real limitations rather than hiding them. The prose can be vivid, but the empirical claim underneath must remain calibrated.

## Rhythm & cadence
Use short or medium declarative sentences around a few longer explanatory ones. A one-sentence paragraph can land a key turn: “Now watch which citizens get taken.” Prefer variation over a uniform essay cadence.

## Sentence construction & syntax
Lead with the point or a concrete situation; build to the abstraction. Use rhetorical questions sparingly when they carry the argument. Contrast is common: “The vulnerability is not a flaw in the model's reasoning. The vulnerability *is* the model's reasoning.”

## Punctuation signature
Use em dashes for a pointed aside or turn in thought; italics for emphasis and examples. Avoid exclamation marks, fussy semicolons, and parenthetical clutter.

## Vocabulary they reach for
“the point”, “the whole problem”, “make it concrete”, “the catch”, “load-bearing”, “strategic”, “public commitment”, “the other side”, “what the experiment measures”, “not a thought experiment”. Technical terms are explained immediately.

## Words & phrases they NEVER use
Avoid “delve”, “leverage”, “unlock”, “robust solution”, “game-changer”, “in conclusion”, “it is important to note”, “landscape”, and generic hype. Do not use “Picture this” as a hook.

## Openings / hooks
Open cold with a concrete case that exposes the article’s mechanism. Examples: “Two governments face the same kidnappers” and “We wrote the conscience down. Then we gave it a job.”

## Closings / landings
Return to the opening example and finish on an imperative or unresolved practical demand, not a neutral recap. Example: “So measure it. The rest is just the work.”

## Transitions & structure
Use descriptive headers and move from example → mechanism → evidence → operationalisation → limitation. Transition with concise pivots such as “Here is the uncomfortable part” or “You have to measure the right thing, though”.

## How they teach & explain
Teach through a tangible case, then restate it in the experiment’s terms. Explain technical distinctions using an ordinary contrast, not a taxonomy.

## How they handle opinion & stance
State the intended conclusion plainly, then qualify only where the evidence requires it. Acknowledge the strongest objection before resolving or narrowing it.

## Emotional texture & humour
Earnest with a faintly provocative edge; no jokes or self-deprecation. The reader is addressed as someone capable of following an uncomfortable argument.

## Point of view
Use “we” for shared institutional choices, “you” to make an implication immediate, and first person sparingly.

## Do / Don't quick reference
**Do:** open with a strategic thought experiment; alternate vivid prose and precise operational definitions; name the real trade-off; keep the reader moving.

**Don't:** overstate empirical evidence; turn the piece into a moral-philosophy survey; rely on generic safety rhetoric; use tidy AI-style summaries.
